A password on the PDF itself protects it wherever it ends up. Here's what a good PDF protection tool should offer in 2027, and how to do it for free with [PDFCharger's Encrypt PDF](/en/tools/encrypt-pdf/).

## What the best free PDF password tool should do in 2027

### Use strong encryption

A password is only as good as the encryption behind it. Older PDF protection methods are weak and can be broken quickly. PDFCharger uses **256-bit AES**, the strongest encryption the PDF format supports.

### Encrypt without uploading

It makes little sense to upload a confidential document to a website in order to protect it. PDFCharger encrypts the file **inside your browser**, so neither the document nor your password is ever sent anywhere.

### Offer two kinds of password

PDFs support two separate passwords, and a good tool lets you use both:

- **User password (open password):** needed to open and read the document at all.
- **Owner password (permissions password):** needed to change the permissions or remove the protection.

### Let you control what people can do

Sometimes you're happy for people to read a document but don't want them to change or copy it. With PDFCharger you can allow or block:

- printing;
- modifying the content;
- copying text and images;
- adding comments;
- filling in forms;
- assembling the document (inserting, deleting or rotating pages);
- content access for accessibility tools.

## Choosing a good password

- **Make it long.** A phrase of four or five random words is stronger and easier to type than a short jumble of symbols.
- **Don't reuse** a password you use for email or banking.
- **Keep a copy safe.** If you forget the open password of a 256-bit AES PDF, there's no practical way to get back in.

## Share the password the right way

The most common mistake is sending the password in the same email as the PDF. Anyone who gets the email gets both. Instead:

- send the password by **text message or a phone call**;
- or agree a password in advance, in person.

## Know what a password does and doesn't do

- **Permissions are a request, not a lock.** Restrictions like "no printing" are respected by standard PDF readers, but they don't encrypt the content on their own. For truly confidential files, always set an **open password**.
- **A password doesn't hide content forever.** Anyone who has the password can read everything.
