## Tips for the most accurate OCR

- **Scan at a decent resolution.** Around 300 DPI is a good balance; very low-resolution scans lose small text.
- **Keep pages straight.** Skewed or curved pages (like a thick book) are harder to read.
- **Pick the right language.** Recognising a French document as English produces odd results with accents.
- **Use enhancement for faded originals**, such as old photocopies or pencil notes.
- **Check the confidence score.** PDFCharger shows the average confidence after recognition; a low score means it's worth proofreading.

## The honest trade-offs

- **Handwriting is hit and miss.** OCR is built for printed text; neat block capitals work better than joined-up handwriting.
- **Higher accuracy takes longer**, especially on long documents and older phones, because the work happens on your device.
- **The language files download the first time** you use a language, so the first run is slower.
- **Complex layouts**, such as newspapers with many columns, may come out in an unexpected reading order in the text export. The searchable PDF keeps everything in place.
